# Cell and molecular biology figures

State the scale and biological context: organism, cell type, compartment and relevant assembly state. Distinguish a molecular structure supported by a structural model from a schematic cartoon. Proteins are dynamic; do not present one conformation as the only state unless the figure is explicitly about that state.

Use ball-and-stick geometry for selected atoms and bonds, a helix primitive for clearly identified helices, and ribbons for protein backbones or nucleic-acid strands. Preserve chain direction and orientation where they are part of the mechanism. The primitives are explanatory, not atomistic simulations or substitutes for a validated structural viewer.

Source structures and numerical dimensions from a cited structure record or publication. Identify the source accession where applicable, the chain or subunit, and any omitted components. Do not infer molecular size, stoichiometry or interaction strength from illustration proportions.

Follow `references/bio.md` for scale declarations, citations and schematic labeling. Use `kit/chem.mjs` for renderable shapes, not to make unsupported claims about molecular conformation.
