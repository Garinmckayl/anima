# Pharmacology and drug figures

Identify the molecule, target, species and assay or clinical context. Distinguish affinity, potency, efficacy, receptor occupancy, exposure and dose; they are related but not interchangeable. A dose–occupancy readout needs a defined model and source data, and must not be presented as a dose recommendation.

Use an authoritative product label for approved use and dosing information, and primary or high-quality pharmacology sources for binding and mechanism. Cite every number, including concentration, dose, occupancy, response and time. State route, units and relevant population or assay conditions.

Show binding as a reversible molecular interaction unless the source supports another mechanism. An agonist, partial agonist, antagonist and inverse agonist do not have equivalent effects; do not use “binding” as a proxy for activation.

Every drug or dosing figure must say it is educational and not medical advice. Never advise an individual to start, stop, select or change a medication or dose.

## Drugs of abuse, stimulants and addiction figures

Show mechanism of action only: the target, what the molecule does to it, and the downstream signalling. Do not show doses, routes, purity, preparation or how a drug feels, and do not present use as an outcome to aim for. Take skeletal formulas from a structure record such as PubChem and check atom counts against the molecular formula.

Separate what is established (a transporter is blocked, a substrate is transported) from what is one leading model of addiction (changes in receptor availability, incentive sensitisation, weaker prefrontal control). Label the model as a model, keep human imaging and rodent work apart, and note that addiction is multifactorial. Add a short pointer to a local health service or helpline.

## Incretin and metabolic drug figures

Take every displayed pharmacokinetic or sequence number from the official product label, with its population, and cite the label version. Show the receptor mechanism, including glucose dependence where the source supports it, but no dose, schedule, weight-change figure or advice on who should take it. Keep preclinical findings (for example GLP-1 receptors in reward regions in rodents) visibly separate from clinical evidence, and say where the clinical evidence is early or mixed.
